How this works

A reviewer relearns by itself whenever one of your reviews contradicts it: the disagreement is recorded, and the named reviewer's rules are rewritten from every case it has got wrong. Rules are added onto the shipped instruction, never instead of it, so the issue codes and difficulty bands it must use cannot be lost. Writing a prompt here by hand turns that off for that reviewer — your text is then never overwritten.

Any change re-names the whole panel, because the name is a fingerprint of the four prompts in force. That is what lets calibration report a repaired reviewer separately from the one it replaced, instead of averaging both into one agreement figure. It also means the name changes often while you review, and figures do not accumulate under one name for long.

Existing verdicts are not rewritten. A saved prompt applies to questions judged from now on. To score the repair, re-judge the bank from Questions, then read the held-back figure on Professor Feedback — not the overall one, which includes the questions you read while writing the new prompt.
