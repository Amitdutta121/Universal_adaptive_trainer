The words, fixed in one place

Question
One approved question in the bank.
Coverage target
One subtopic at one difficulty. 66 of them here.
Gap
A coverage target holding fewer questions than the target count.
Candidate
A newly generated question, not yet validated or approved.

No "boxes", "slots", "cells to fill" or "queued items" anywhere on the page. Those were mine and they were implementation words leaking into the interface.

Three things worth arguing about

  • "Up to 12 questions", not "12 questions needed." A question may claim up to three subtopics, so one generation can close a gap in three rows at once. Summing the deficits gives an upper bound; the true minimum could be a third of it. That's why gaps is the unit everywhere and questions is the hedge.
  • The target of 3 is a setting, not a law. It is MIN_QUESTIONS_PER_CELL, and build_coverage_report already takes it as a parameter. Every 2 / 3 and ✓ 6 on this page is derived from it — set it to 10 and most of the green turns amber. The page states the number rather than assuming everyone knows it.
  • Covered cells stay as a faint ✓ 5, never an em-dash. A dash reads as "no data", which is the one thing a covered cell is not. In gaps-only mode whole clean rows drop out; the surviving rows keep their ticks so the row still reads as a row.

Still open

  • What "Generate candidates" actually opens. Per ADR-031 the generator picks its own topic and subtopics from a chunk you choose. So this button leads to a chunk picker, not a generation run — and something has to rank chunks by how likely they are to teach the selected subtopics. That ranking does not exist yet and is the real work behind this screen.
  • Sort order of "Needs attention". Currently worst-first by empty targets, then by gap count. Taxonomy order is the alternative and is more predictable term to term.
  • Whether a professor can raise the target per topic. A capstone topic may deserve 6; an introductory one 2. The report already accepts a single minimum_per_cell; per-topic would be a schema change.
