Adaptive practice questions, built from your own textbook.

Upload the book you teach from. Adaptive Trainer drafts a topic outline from it and questions for each subtopic, runs every question through automated checks and four judge prompts, and puts it in front of you to approve, edit or reject. Your decisions are kept: they become rules the generator follows next time, and they measure how often each judge agrees with you. Students join with a code and practise the questions you approved, and the next question always comes from the subtopic they are weakest in.

What students get

A join code, no account

You open a class and share its code (for example PY7-K4Q). Students type the code, their name and school email, and start. You can restrict a class to the students on your roster.

Mastery per topic

Mastery of each topic is estimated with Bayesian knowledge tracing after every answer. The next question is picked from the student's weakest subtopic, at a difficulty that follows their mastery. After each answer they see the explanation and how their mastery moved.

What you see

Each student's mastery per topic over time, their weakest subtopics, and the class average (58% in the sample course). Export the roster as CSV.

Limits

  • Introductory Python is the subject it was built for: all seven question types, with generated code actually run. For other subjects (statistics, biology) it writes multiple-choice and true/false questions only.
  • Books are PDF or structured JSON. A PDF with no table of contents still imports, with its chapters guessed and marked for you to check. EPUB, Markdown and HTML are refused.
  • The judges advise; they don't approve anything. Nothing reaches students until you have approved it and frozen it into a set.
  • Generation and judging use a language model and cost money. Each workspace has a monthly spending cap, and you see the spend per course.
